Paper Video
TL;DR: Passive bistable suction cups generate strong adhesion without continuous power or large application forces. A tracked robot automatically engages and disengages the cups, climbs smooth surfaces in multiple orientations, and carries an additional 7.940 kg payload at a 2.25 payload-to-weight ratio.

Project overview

Wall-climbing robots could automate hazardous tasks such as window washing, inspection, maintenance, and construction, but conventional systems trade payload capacity against energy use and adhesion force. This work introduces a track-based robot with custom passive bistable suction cups. Each cup has engaged and disengaged stable configurations: a small snap-through motion creates the pressure difference needed for adhesion, while the tread system handles engagement, seal breaking, and reset during each revolution. The result is an energy-efficient climbing platform that does not require continuous power to remain attached.
